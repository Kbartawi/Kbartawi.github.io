# Product review standard

The portfolio covers AI products for customer operations and personal workflows, plus a game in preproduction. Each project has its own audience and development stage. This document defines how that progress is described and reviewed.

## What each project should explain

| Question | Evidence to include |
| --- | --- |
| Who is it for? | Intended user, buyer and the task they need to complete. |
| What does it do? | A concrete workflow, real product screenshots and a demo where available. |
| Does it work? | Technical readiness, the environment checked and remaining acceptance gaps. |
| Is it useful? | Output quality, effort, time, corrections and actual user feedback. |
| Why choose it? | A specific advantage for that customer, compared with relevant alternatives. |
| What comes next? | One testable milestone that reduces a material product or commercial uncertainty. |

## Technical readiness

Keep implementation, automated tests, hosted availability and live workflow acceptance separate. A successful build does not prove a customer can complete the task. A sample interface does not establish that its production integrations work.

Use **Passed**, **Failed** or **Not checked**, with a date and evidence. For a material change, retain the baseline, the observed result and relevant regression checks. Inspect actual rendered behavior for interface changes.

## Commercial readiness

Separate intended users and proposed pricing from customers, revenue and repeat use. Report measured outcomes only when evidence is available. Unknown demand, retention or production economics remain explicit validation questions.

Automated walkthroughs can identify friction and failures. They do not count as customer interviews, willingness to pay or proof of product-market fit. The next commercial milestone should specify the customer, task and evidence needed.

## Improvement cycle

Test a representative task, inspect the result, study a relevant alternative, choose one worthwhile change, verify it and retain the learning. Prioritize failures in the core workflow before adding features. Keep changes isolated and reviewable, and preserve active development work.

Study direct competitors and the manual alternative for the same customer task. Use exemplary products to understand onboarding, interaction quality, messaging and customer proof. Record sources and distinguish observed facts from hypotheses; familiarity with a large company is not evidence that its approach fits this product.

## Product communication

Use professional, human language. Explain the customer, purpose, current capability and next milestone. Keep the website, GitHub bio and project READMEs consistent without forcing identical copy onto different products.

Use **Problem** when there is a customer problem and **Concept** for exploratory work. Present Dubai Game as a game in preproduction. Label concept artwork, fictional sample data and real product captures accurately. Preserve each product’s identity within a consistent visual system.

Avoid vague slogans, casual hobby framing, invented traction and unsupported claims of superiority. A useful description should help someone understand the product and assess the evidence.

## Reference methods

- [Anthropic’s agent evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents): final outcomes, regression checks and calibrated human review.
- [OpenAI’s in-house data agent](https://openai.com/index/inside-our-in-house-data-agent/): systematic evaluation of an evolving product.
- [Notion customer stories](https://www.notion.com/customers) and [Salesforce’s sales products](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/): examples to study for customer context, product explanations and proof.
